Best drives by research bracket

What is plotted
Every drive design as its x6 bank — what ships actually mount — except the Orion-type drives, which only exist as x1. Alien drives are left out: they arrive from captured hulls, not from research. Bold entries in the brackets match or beat the Helicon Drive on both axes.
Research
The full prereq closure of the drive alone, with shared prereqs counted once. The reactor and radiator are a reference for working out mass, not a shopping list, so their own research is not added in.
Package mass
The drive, plus a power plant sized to its draw (draw × the plant's t/GW — the cheapest plant to research of the class the drive demands that can carry the load), plus Dusty Plasma radiators for the waste heat the plant does not convert, (1 − plant efficiency) × draw at 18 kW/kg. Open-cycle drives radiate through the nozzle and carry none.
Propellant
The rocket equation over the hull mass and Δv you set, rounded up to whole 100 t tanks the way the ship builder sells them. Dry mass is your hull plus the package, and acceleration is quoted with full tanks. The tanks themselves, and the hull, are not massed.
Supply cost
A trip priced in months of a mid-game faction's output — 5,000 t of water and metals, 2,000 t of volatiles, 1,000 t of noble metals, 300 t of fissiles, and antimatter at a twentieth of that — so 3,000 t of common volatiles counts as the real bill it is.
Best and best expensive
Best scores acceleration — credited from 2 milli-g, where the ship can manoeuvre at all, up to 10 milli-g, past which more of it stops being worth paying for — against the supply months of the trip. Best expensive is the most powerful drive in the bracket when that is a different one. Drives under 2 milli-g, or needing more than three times the ship's mass in propellant, are dropped: a chemical rocket holds the frontier on thrust alone, but wanting 24 times its own mass in fuel settles it. All of this is recomputed for the ship above, because all of it changes with it.
Dot area
Clamped to the 5th–95th percentile of whichever measure it shows, so a single outlier cannot flatten the rest.
