Best drives by research bracket

Pareto-optimal for fuel efficiency and thrust · bold drives meet or exceed Helicon in both · best is the drive worth flying on the ship above — acceleration you can actually use, against what the trip costs to fuel — and best expensive the most powerful one in the bracket when that is a different drive

Research totals are the full prereq closure of the drive alone, with shared prereqs counted once — the reactor and radiator below are a reference for mass, not a shopping list, so their own research is not added in. Package mass is the drive plus a power plant sized to its draw (draw × the plant's t/GW; the plant chosen is the cheapest to research that is of the class the drive demands and can carry the load) plus Dusty Plasma radiators for the waste heat the plant does not convert — (1 − plant efficiency) × drive draw, at 18 kW/kg. The supply figure prices a trip in months of a mid-game faction's output — 5,000 t of water and metals, 2,000 t of volatiles, 1,000 t of noble metals, 300 t of fissiles, and antimatter at a twentieth of that — so 3,000 t of common volatiles counts as the real bill it is. No absolute figure decides what is affordable: a bracket's best is whichever drive scores highest on acceleration — credited on a log scale from 2 milli-g, where it is barely flyable, up to 10 milli-g, past which more of it stops being worth paying for — against the supply months the trip costs. Below 0.2 milli-g a drive is dropped: it cannot manoeuvre that ship at all. Drives that would need more than three times the ship's mass in propellant are dropped from the bracket entirely — a chemical rocket holds its place on the frontier forever on thrust alone, but wanting 24 times its own mass in fuel settles the question. All of this is recomputed for the ship and Δv above, because all of it changes with them. Open-cycle drives radiate through the nozzle and carry none. Propellant is the rocket equation over the hull mass and Δv you set, rounded up to whole 100 t tanks the way the ship builder makes you buy them — dry mass is your hull plus the propulsion package, and acceleration is quoted with full tanks. The tanks themselves, and the hull, are not massed. Dot area is clamped to the 5th–95th percentile of whichever measure it shows, so one outlier cannot flatten the rest. Alien drives are left out — they arrive from captured hulls, not from research. Drives are plotted as their x6 variants — what ships actually mount — except the Orion-type drives, which only exist as x1.
